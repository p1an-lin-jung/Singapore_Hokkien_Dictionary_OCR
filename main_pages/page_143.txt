【痴哥伯】  [tsʰi¹⁻⁶ ko¹⁻⁶ peʔ⁷]
指好色之徒，老色鬼‖闽南话常用“猪哥[ti(tu)¹⁻⁶ ko¹]”讥讽蔑称好色者。疑“痴哥”是源自“猪哥”，但“猪”借粤语“猪”的[tsy¹]音讹变过来的。新加坡英语吸收该词。《SE》48页：“chee-ko pek Cheeko come from the English word cheeky (see the next entry), while pek (pronounced pake) is an abbreviation of the Hokkien word ah pek, meaning 'uncle'(in the SgE sense;see Ah pek, auntie). Together, they refer to 'dirty old man'. The Cantonese version is hum sub loh.”又：“cheeky In StdE,'if you describe a person or their behaviour as cheeky, you think that they are slightly rude or disrespectful but in a charming or amusing way. The boy was cheeky and casual'(Cobuild). It is therefore most frequently used of children. In SgE, however, it may also be used far more disapprovingly, with the meaning 'forward'. It may thus be used by girls and women to describe older men who, by unwanted attentions, are getting fresh with them.”注：如果说《SE》认为新加坡闽南话的“cheekopeh”相当于粤语的“hum sub loh”（见《广州方言词典》286页：“【咸湿】ham²səp⁷ 淫秽，下流。用于男性：~佬｜~鬼（色鬼）”），那么，《SE》将“cheekopek”解释为“dirty old man”，理解为污秽、下流的老头或色鬼会更精确
